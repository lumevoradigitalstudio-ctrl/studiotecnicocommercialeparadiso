# Turdilli with fig honey (fried Christmas gnocchi)

*Turdilli al miele di fichi · turdiddri · Cosenza · Christmas*

Around Cosenza, Christmas smells of frying and honey. Turdilli are little gnocchi of dough made with wine and olive oil, ridged on a basket or a grater, fried and then tossed in warm fig honey, dark and thick, which makes them shine like glass. The dough has no eggs and no yeast: it is the plain dough of country cooking, made festive by the honey. They are made in December and stay on the table until Epiphany, on a large plate from which everyone takes with their fingers. Fig honey is hard to find outside Calabria: further down you'll find how to replace it and, in recipe 16, how to make it at home.

**Makes:** about 60 pieces · **Prep:** 1 hour · **Cook:** 30 minutes · **Rest:** 30 minutes · **Total:** 2 hours · **Difficulty:** medium

## Ingredients

**For the dough**
- 500 g (1.1 lb) Italian 00 flour, or plain/all-purpose flour
- 150 ml (¾ cup) red wine (or muscat)
- 100 ml (½ cup) mild extra virgin olive oil
- grated zest of 1 unwaxed orange
- 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon
- 1 pinch salt

**For frying**
- 1 l (4¼ cups) high-oleic sunflower oil, or olive oil

**For the glaze**
- 300 g (11 oz) fig honey
- 2 tablespoons water

**Equipment:** a gnocchi board, a clean wicker basket or the coarse side of a grater; kitchen thermometer.

## Method

1. Put the flour in a large bowl with the salt, cinnamon and orange zest. Mix.
2. Heat the wine and oil in a small pan until hot to the touch (about 60 °C (140 °F)), without boiling.
3. Pour the hot liquid over the flour and stir straight away with a wooden spoon.
4. When the dough can be handled, knead it on the work surface for 5 minutes, until smooth, firm and not sticky.
5. Cover with a tea towel and leave to rest for 30 minutes.
6. Cut off a piece of dough and roll it into a rope 1.5 cm (⅝ in) thick. Cut it into 3 cm (1¼ in) pieces.
7. Press each piece with your thumb onto the gnocchi board (or the basket) and roll it towards you: it should curl up on itself and stay ridged outside. Put the turdilli on a floured tray.
8. Heat the oil in a deep pot to 170 °C (340 °F). Without a thermometer: a small piece of dough dropped in should rise straight to the surface with lots of bubbles.
9. Fry 10-12 turdilli at a time for 4-5 minutes, turning them, until deep golden brown and they sound hollow when tapped with the slotted spoon.
10. Drain on paper towels. Before each batch wait for the oil to return to 170 °C (340 °F).
11. Let the turdilli cool for at least 15 minutes.
12. In a wide pan warm the honey with the water over low heat, until runny and just bubbling at the edges, about 2-3 minutes.
13. Turn off the heat, add the turdilli and turn them with a spoon for 1-2 minutes, until glossy on every side.
14. Arrange them on a serving plate. The next day they are even better.

## Tips
- The hot liquid makes the dough more crumbly: don't skip step 2.
- Fry a few at a time, so the oil doesn't cool and the turdilli don't soak it up.
- If the honey thickens too much in the pan, put it back on the heat for 30 seconds.

## Common mistakes
- Don't fry over too high a heat: they darken outside and stay raw inside.
- Don't boil the honey for long: it turns into hard caramel.
- Don't put hot turdilli into the honey: they soften and lose their crunch.

## Local variations
- Instead of red wine, some families use white wine, muscat or vermouth.
- In some homes eggs or yeast are added to the dough `[TO BE VERIFIED: how common by area]`.
- Fig honey can be replaced by bee honey, and they are sometimes scattered with coloured sprinkles.

## Storage
In a tin, in a cool, dry place, for 2 weeks.

## Substitutions
- **Fig honey:** use chestnut honey, for a darker taste, or wildflower honey, or home-made fig honey (recipe 16).
- **Gnocchi board:** the back of a fork works.

## Allergens (14 EU allergens)
Gluten (wheat flour), sulphites (wine).

## PDO and PGI
None required.

## Wine
A sweet Calabrian wine, such as **Greco di Bianco DOC**.
