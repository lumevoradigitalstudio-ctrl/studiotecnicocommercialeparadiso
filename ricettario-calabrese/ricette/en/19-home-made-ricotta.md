# Home-made ricotta (fresh lemon cheese)

*Ricotta di casa · all of Calabria · all year*

True ricotta is made from the whey left over after cheesemaking, and that is how shepherds make it, with sheep's or goat's milk. A city kitchen has no whey. But in a few minutes you can make a similar fresh cheese, soft and sweet, by heating whole milk and curdling it with lemon juice. It isn't the shepherd's ricotta, and this book says so plainly, but on the table it comes very close. It is lovely warm with a drizzle of fig honey (recipe 16), on bread, in pasta fillings and in desserts. All you need is good milk and a thermometer.

**Makes:** about 400 g (14 oz) · **Prep:** 10 minutes · **Cook:** 15 minutes · **Rest:** 30 minutes-2 hours · **Difficulty:** easy

## Ingredients

- 2 l (2 quarts) fresh whole pasteurised milk (not UHT)
- 200 ml (¾ cup) fresh cream, optional, for a creamier cheese
- 5 g (0.2 oz) fine salt
- 70-80 ml (¼-¼ cup) strained lemon juice, about 2 lemons

**Equipment:** heavy-based stainless-steel pot, kitchen thermometer, sieve, cheesecloth or a clean cotton cloth (or ricotta baskets), slotted spoon.

## Method

1. Pour the milk, cream and salt into the pot.
2. Heat over medium-low heat, stirring now and then with a wooden spoon so the milk doesn't stick.
3. Bring it to 85-90 °C (185-194 °F) `[TO BE VERIFIED: temperature]`: small bubbles appear around the edge and steam rises, but the milk must not boil.
4. Turn off the heat and pour in the lemon juice in a thin stream, stirring gently, once only.
5. Leave it for 10 minutes without touching it. White curds form, and the liquid around them turns yellow-green and almost clear.
6. If the liquid is still white and milky, add another spoonful of lemon juice and wait 5 minutes.
7. Line the sieve with the wet, wrung-out cheesecloth and set it over a bowl.
8. Lift the curds out with the slotted spoon and put them in the sieve.
9. Let them drain for 30 minutes for a soft cheese, up to 2 hours for a firmer one.
10. Serve it straight away, still warm, or put it in the fridge.

## Tips
- Fresh whole milk gives the best result: long-life UHT milk curdles poorly.
- Don't throw away the whey that's left: use it to make bread dough or to cook pasta.

## Common mistakes
- Don't boil the milk: the curds turn hard and rubbery.
- Don't stir after adding the lemon: the curds break up and slip through the cloth.

## Local variations
- White wine vinegar can be used instead of lemon, in the same amount.
- True sheep's or goat's ricotta is sold by Calabrian dairies. Fresh, it is excellent with honey, and it is used in many sweets in this book.

## Storage
In the fridge, in a closed container, for 3 days.

## Substitutions
The method works with any fresh, pasteurised whole milk, including goat's milk.

## Allergens (14 EU allergens)
Milk.

## PDO and PGI
None.

## Wine
With fig honey, a sweet wine such as **Greco di Bianco DOC**.
