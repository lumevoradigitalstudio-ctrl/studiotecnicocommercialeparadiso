# Bread balls in tomato sauce

*Polpette di pane · purpetti 'i pani · all of Calabria · all year*

In a home kitchen, bread is never thrown away. Bread a few days old, hard but still good, is soaked, squeezed and turned into polpette with eggs, pecorino, garlic and parsley. They are fried and then cooked in tomato sauce, which softens them and gives them flavour, or simply eaten fried. They make a main course that costs very little and that children love. Recipes like this are found all over southern Italy: in Calabria the mixture is the same as for the Sunday meatballs, just without the meat. This recipe uses tomato sauce, the most common way to serve them.

**Serves:** 4 (about 20 balls) · **Prep:** 25 minutes · **Cook:** 35 minutes · **Rest:** 15 minutes · **Total:** 1 hour 15 minutes · **Difficulty:** easy

## Ingredients

**For the bread balls**
- 300 g (11 oz) stale durum wheat bread, crusts removed
- 3 medium eggs
- 80 g (3 oz) grated pecorino
- 1 garlic clove, finely chopped
- 2 tablespoons chopped parsley
- 1 pinch ground chilli, optional
- salt and pepper
- dried breadcrumbs, if needed
- 500 ml (2 cups) oil for frying (olive oil or high-oleic sunflower oil)

**For the sauce**
- 2 tablespoons extra virgin olive oil
- ½ onion, chopped
- 700 g (1.5 lb) tomato passata
- 4-5 basil leaves
- salt

## Method

1. Break the bread into pieces and soak it in cold water for 5 minutes, until soft right through.
2. Squeeze it well with your hands, a handful at a time, until no more water comes out.
3. Crumble it into a bowl and leave it for 15 minutes: it dries a little more.
4. Add the eggs, pecorino, garlic, parsley, chilli, salt and pepper. Mix with your hands.
5. The mixture should be soft but firm. If it is too wet, add breadcrumbs a spoonful at a time.
6. With wet hands, shape balls the size of a walnut and flatten them slightly.
7. Meanwhile make the sauce: heat the oil in a wide pan and cook the onion over low heat for 5 minutes, until translucent.
8. Add the passata and a pinch of salt, and cook for 15 minutes over low heat.
9. Heat the frying oil to 170 °C (340 °F) in a deep frying pan.
10. Fry 5-6 balls at a time for 2-3 minutes per side, until golden.
11. Drain them on paper towels.
12. Put them in the sauce and cook for 10 minutes over low heat, turning them gently once.
13. Add the basil and serve hot.

## Tips
- Durum wheat bread holds together better than soft white bread.
- Make the sauce while the bread rests: it saves time.

## Common mistakes
- Don't leave the bread wet: the balls fall apart in the oil.
- Don't turn them too often in the sauce: they are soft and break.

## Local variations
- They can be eaten simply fried and hot, with a salad.

## Storage
In the fridge, in their sauce, for 2 days. They reheat well in a pan over low heat.

## Substitutions
Any firm, dense bread a few days old works. Instead of pecorino, any hard aged cheese.

## Allergens (14 EU allergens)
Gluten (bread, breadcrumbs), eggs, milk (pecorino).

## PDO and PGI
Optional: **Pecorino Crotonese PDO** instead of ordinary pecorino.

## Wine
A **Savuto DOC red**.
