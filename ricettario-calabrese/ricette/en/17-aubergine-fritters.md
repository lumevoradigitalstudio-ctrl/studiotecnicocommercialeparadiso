# Aubergine (eggplant) fritters

*Polpette di melanzane · purpetti 'i mulingiani · all of Calabria · summer*

Aubergine polpette are the "meatballs" of those who had no meat. With aubergines from the garden, stale bread, an egg and pecorino you make a dish that is never missing from Calabrian homes in summer. It is listed among Calabria's traditional food products `[TO BE VERIFIED]`. The aubergines are cooked, squeezed dry, mixed and fried until golden outside and soft inside. They are eaten hot, warm or cold the next day, on their own, in a sandwich or simmered in tomato sauce. The only secret is to squeeze the aubergines well, because water makes them fall apart in the pan.

**Serves:** 4 (about 20 fritters) · **Prep:** 30 minutes · **Cook:** 35 minutes · **Rest:** 15 minutes · **Total:** 1 hour 20 minutes · **Difficulty:** easy

## Ingredients

- 800 g (1.8 lb) aubergines, 2 large ones
- 120 g (4 oz) stale bread, crusts removed
- 2 medium eggs
- 70 g (2½ oz) grated pecorino
- 1 garlic clove, finely chopped
- 1 tablespoon chopped parsley
- 6-8 mint or basil leaves, torn
- 1 pinch ground chilli, optional
- salt and pepper
- 50 g (2 oz) dried breadcrumbs, plus more as needed
- 500 ml (2 cups) oil for frying (olive oil or high-oleic sunflower oil)

## Method

1. Wash the aubergines, remove the stems and peel them. Cut them into 3 cm (1¼ in) cubes.
2. Bring 2 litres of salted water to the boil and cook the aubergines for 8-10 minutes, until soft when pierced with a fork.
3. Drain them and let them cool in the colander for 15 minutes.
4. Squeeze them hard, a handful at a time, or wrapped in a tea towel, until almost no more water comes out. They should become a dry mass.
5. Soak the bread in cold water for 2 minutes and squeeze it well.
6. In a bowl, mash the aubergines and bread with a fork.
7. Add the eggs, pecorino, garlic, parsley, mint, chilli, salt and pepper. Mix with your hands.
8. Add breadcrumbs a spoonful at a time, until the mixture is soft but doesn't stick to your hands.
9. Fry a small test fritter. If it falls apart in the oil, add another spoonful of breadcrumbs.
10. With wet hands, shape fritters the size of a large walnut and flatten them slightly.
11. Heat the oil to 170 °C (340 °F) in a deep frying pan.
12. Fry 5-6 fritters at a time for 3-4 minutes per side, until golden.
13. Drain them on paper towels and serve hot or warm.

## Tips
- Bread that is a few days old holds the mixture together better than fresh bread.
- Wet hands make shaping easier and stop the mixture sticking.

## Common mistakes
- Don't skip the squeezing: it decides whether the fritters hold together.
- Don't crowd the pan: the oil cools and the fritters soak it up.

## Local variations
- **In sauce:** after frying, simmer them for 10 minutes in a simple tomato sauce.
- **Baked:** place them on a tray lined with baking paper, brush with oil and bake at 200 °C (390 °F) for 25 minutes, turning halfway.
- Instead of boiling, some families roast the aubergines whole in the oven, then scoop them out.

## Storage
In the fridge, in a closed container, for 2 days. They can be frozen after frying and reheated in the oven at 180 °C (355 °F) for 10-15 minutes.

## Substitutions
Any long or round aubergines work. Instead of pecorino you can use any hard aged cheese, such as Parmesan.

## Allergens (14 EU allergens)
Gluten (bread, breadcrumbs), eggs, milk (pecorino).

## PDO and PGI
Optional: **Pecorino Crotonese PDO** instead of ordinary pecorino.

## Wine
A fresh **Cirò Rosato DOC**.
