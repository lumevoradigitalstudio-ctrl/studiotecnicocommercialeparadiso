# Aubergines (eggplant) in oil

*Melanzane sott'olio · mulingiani sutt'ogghiu · Catanzaro, common across the region · late summer*

At the end of summer, when the garden gives more aubergines than anyone can eat, Calabrians fill jars for the winter. The aubergines are cut into strips, salted and pressed to release their bitter water. Then they go through vinegar and end up under oil with garlic, chilli and oregano. On warm bread, next to a piece of cheese, they make a meal. This recipe follows the Italian National Institute of Health (ISS) guidelines for home preserves, because vegetables in oil, if badly prepared, can cause botulism. With the right acidity and processed jars, the tradition stays intact and the risk does not.

**Makes:** 4-5 jars of 250 ml (from 2 kg (4.4 lb) of aubergines) · **Prep:** 1 hour 30 minutes · **Cook:** 40 minutes · **Rest:** 12 hours + 10-15 days · **Difficulty:** medium

## Before you start: safety

Oil does not protect against botulism: it shuts out the air, and the bacterium thrives precisely without air. Protection comes from the **acidity** of the vinegar and from **processing** the jars. Follow the amounts without reducing the vinegar (ISS guidelines, see the sources file).

- Use white wine vinegar with **at least 6% acidity**, as stated on the label.
- Use undamaged glass jars and **new lids** every time.
- Keep the aubergines always covered with oil, with 1-2 cm (⅜-¾ in) of headspace below the rim.

## Ingredients

- 2 kg (4.4 lb) long aubergines, firm and glossy
- 60 g (2 oz) coarse salt
- 1 l (4¼ cups) white wine vinegar at 6%
- 500 ml (2 cups) water
- 4 garlic cloves, sliced
- 2-3 dried hot chillies, broken up, or 1 teaspoon chilli flakes
- 2 tablespoons dried oregano
- about 600 ml (2½ cups) extra virgin olive oil

**Equipment:** large colander, a weight (a pot full of water), clean tea towel, 250 ml (1 cup) jars with new lids, tall pot for processing.

## Method

1. Wash and dry the aubergines. Remove the stems.
2. Cut the aubergines into slices 1 cm (⅜ in) thick, then into strips 1 cm (⅜ in) wide. Don't peel them: the skin holds the strips together.
3. Layer the strips in the colander, sprinkling each layer with coarse salt.
4. Cover with a plate and put a weight of 3-4 kg (6.6-8.8 lb) on top. Leave to drain for 12 hours in a cool place. A dark liquid should collect underneath.
5. Take the aubergines a handful at a time and squeeze them hard, until almost no more liquid comes out.
6. Bring the vinegar and water to the boil in a stainless-steel pot.
7. Plunge the aubergines into the boiling liquid in two batches, together with the sliced garlic. Counting from when the boil returns, cook for 3-4 minutes: the strips should turn pale and flexible.
8. Drain and spread the aubergines on a clean tea towel. Let them cool for 1 hour, then squeeze them again in the towel.
9. Season them in a bowl with the chilli and oregano, mixing with clean hands or two forks.
10. Fill the jars in layers, pressing with a spoon to remove air. Stop 2 cm (¾ in) below the rim.
11. Pour in oil to cover the aubergines well. Wait 30 minutes for the oil to settle into the gaps. Top up to 1-2 cm (⅜-¾ in) below the rim.
12. Wipe the rims with paper towel and close with the new lids.
13. Stand the jars in a tall pot, separated by a tea towel. Cover them with cold water, which must come a few centimetres above them.
14. Bring to a rolling boil and from that moment count 20 minutes. If the boil stops, start again from zero.
15. Turn off the heat and let the jars cool in the water. Then dry them and check the lid: pressing the centre, it must not click.
16. Store in a cool, dark place. Check the jars every day for 10-15 days: the oil must stay clear, with no rising bubbles.

## Tips
- Choose firm aubergines with few seeds: they give firmer strips.
- Squeeze them well twice. Leftover water dilutes the acidity and clouds the oil.
- Use a mild extra virgin olive oil: a very bitter one overpowers the aubergine.

## Common mistakes
- Don't put raw garlic, herbs or fresh chilli in the jar: they carry spores from the soil. The garlic is blanched in vinegar with the aubergines; chilli and oregano are used dried.
- Don't reuse lids: a used seal doesn't close properly.
- Never taste a suspicious preserve.

## When to throw a jar away
Throw it away **without tasting** if the lid is bulging, if you see rising bubbles, cloudy oil or foam, or if you notice a strange smell or a hiss of gas when you open it. Botulinum toxin may have no smell or taste: when in doubt, throw it out.

## Local variations
- In many homes dried mint is used instead of oregano, or together with it.

## Storage
- Closed, processed jars: in a cool, dark place, use within 2 months.
- Opened jar: in the fridge, with the aubergines always covered with oil, use within 7-10 days.

## Substitutions
Any long, dark aubergine works. Avoid large round ones, which have many seeds. Calabrian oregano can be replaced by good dried Greek or Sicilian oregano. Outside Italy, also check your national home-canning guidance (for example, the USDA in the United States).

## Allergens (14 EU allergens)
Sulphites, if present in the wine vinegar (read the label).

## PDO and PGI
Optional: a Calabrian PDO extra virgin olive oil (**Lametia PDO**, **Bruzio PDO**, **Alto Crotonese PDO**) or **Olio di Calabria PGI**.

## Wine
With an antipasto of aubergines in oil, bread and cheese: a **Cirò Bianco DOC**.
