# Dried chilli, chilli powder and chilli oil

*Peperoncino essiccato, in polvere e in olio · all of Calabria · late summer and autumn*

In Calabria there is always chilli. At the end of summer, strings of red chillies hang from balconies and kitchen beams until they are dry and light as paper. Then they are crumbled by hand, ground into powder or put into oil. Half the recipes in this book use a pinch or a handful of it. This page explains how to dry chillies in the air or in the oven, how to grind them and how to make a safe chilli oil. For the oil, use only completely dry chilli: fresh chilli in oil carries a risk of botulism.

**Makes:** about 150 g (5½ oz) of dried chilli from 1 kg (2.2 lb) fresh · **Prep:** 1 hour · **Drying:** 2-4 weeks in the air or 8-12 hours in the oven · **Difficulty:** easy

## Ingredients

- 1 kg (2.2 lb) fresh hot chillies, ripe and red, with their stems
- for the oil: 20 g (0.7 oz) dried chilli, broken into pieces, and 500 ml (2 cups) extra virgin olive oil

**Equipment:** gloves, large needle and string (for strings), oven trays and baking paper (for the oven), spice grinder or mortar, clean, dry bottle or jar.

## Method

**In the air (strings)**
1. Put on gloves. Wash the chillies and dry them very well, one by one.
2. Discard any with spots or soft parts.
3. Thread the string through the stem of each chilli with the needle, leaving a little space between them.
4. Hang the strings in a dry, ventilated, shaded place. Direct sun bleaches the chilli.
5. Wait 2-4 weeks. The chilli is ready when it is light and brittle and rattles when you shake it, because the seeds have come loose inside.

**In the oven**
1. Put on gloves. Wash and dry the chillies, then cut them in half lengthwise.
2. Lay them on trays lined with baking paper, cut side up.
3. Dry them at 50-60 °C (122-140 °F), fan setting, with the door held ajar with a wooden spoon, for 8-12 hours.
4. They are ready when they snap between your fingers. If they bend, leave them longer.

**The powder**
1. Remove the stems from the completely dry chillies.
2. For a milder powder remove the seeds too. For a hotter one, leave them.
3. Grind in short bursts in the grinder, or pound in the mortar, until you reach the texture you want. Don't breathe in the powder when you open the grinder.
4. Store in a closed glass jar, in the dark.

**The chilli oil**
1. Break up the completely dry chilli and put it in the clean, dry bottle.
2. Heat the oil to 60-70 °C (warm to hot, it must not smoke) and pour it over the chilli through a funnel.
3. Let it cool, close and leave for 1 week in the dark, shaking now and then.
4. Strain, if you want a clear oil, and store in the dark.

## Tips
- Wash your hands well even if you wore gloves, and don't touch your eyes.
- For a bright-coloured powder, dry the chilli in the shade and keep it in the dark.

## Common mistakes
- Don't put fresh or not completely dry chilli in oil: the water it contains creates a botulism risk.
- Don't store dried chilli in a damp place: it goes mouldy.

## Local variations
- Hot chilli and dried sweet pepper are ground together for a milder powder, like the one used for cured meats and 'nduja.
- Some homes make a fresh chilli paste under oil. It carries a risk: if you make it, keep it in the fridge and eat it within a few days, or follow the rules for acidified preserves (vinegar and processing) `[TO BE VERIFIED against the ISS guidelines]`.

## Storage
- Dried chilli, whole or ground: in closed glass jars, dark and dry, for about a year.
- Chilli oil with dried chilli: in the dark, in a closed bottle, for 2-3 months `[TO BE VERIFIED]`. Always use a dry spoon.

## Substitutions
Any thin-skinned hot chilli that dries well works, such as cayenne.

## Allergens (14 EU allergens)
None.

## PDO and PGI
Optional for the oil: a **Calabrian PDO** extra virgin olive oil or **Olio di Calabria PGI**.

## Wine
Not applicable: this is a preserve.
