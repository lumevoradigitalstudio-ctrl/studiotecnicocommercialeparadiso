# Cuddrurieddri (fried potato dough rings)

*Cuddrurieddri, cullurielli · Cosenza · 8 December, Immaculate Conception*

Around Cosenza, the Feast of the Immaculate Conception on 8 December is not celebrated without cuddrurieddri. They are rings of leavened dough, soft and a little irregular, fried in oil and eaten hot, lightly salted. Many families put boiled potatoes in the dough, which makes it softer. Others say the oldest recipe had none. They are eaten plain, or filled with a piece of anchovy or caciocavallo cheese. On 8 December kitchens smell of frying, and dozens of rings are fried one after another. They are made again until Christmas, because there are never enough. The dough is very soft and is handled with oiled hands.

**Makes:** about 15 rings · **Prep:** 45 minutes · **Cook:** 40 minutes · **Rest:** 2-3 hours · **Total:** about 4 hours · **Difficulty:** medium

## Ingredients

- 200 g (7 oz) yellow-fleshed potatoes, weighed raw
- 450 g (16 oz) Italian 00 flour, or plain/all-purpose flour
- 50 g (2 oz) semola rimacinata (re-milled durum wheat semolina)
- about 220 ml (1 cup) lukewarm water, about 30 °C (86 °F)
- 7 g (0.2 oz) fresh yeast, or 3 g dried yeast
- 1 teaspoon sugar
- 8 g (0.3 oz) fine salt, plus a little to finish
- 1 l (4¼ cups) oil for frying (olive oil or high-oleic sunflower oil)
- extra virgin olive oil for your hands

**For the filling (optional)**
- 6 anchovy fillets in oil, halved, or 100 g (3½ oz) caciocavallo cheese in sticks

## Method

1. Boil the potatoes in their skins, starting in cold water, for 25-30 minutes from the boil, until tender.
2. Peel and mash them while still hot. Let them cool.
3. Dissolve the yeast and sugar in 100 ml (½ cup) of the lukewarm water.
4. In a bowl, mix the flour, semolina and potatoes. Add the water with the yeast, then the rest of the water a little at a time, kneading by hand.
5. Add the salt and work the dough in the bowl for 8-10 minutes. It should be very soft, elastic and sticky.
6. Cover with a tea towel and leave to rise for 2-3 hours in a warm place, until more than doubled and full of bubbles.
7. Oil your hands and oil a plate.
8. Heat the frying oil to 175-180 °C (345-355 °F) in a deep pot.
9. Take a piece of dough the size of a mandarin, stretch it into a rope 20 cm (7¾ in) long and close it into a ring, pressing the ends together. If you want it filled, wrap the dough around the piece of anchovy or caciocavallo, then close it into a ring.
10. Put the ring on the oiled plate, then slide it into the oil. Fry 2-3 rings at a time.
11. Cook them for 2-3 minutes per side, until puffed and golden.
12. Drain them on paper towels and salt them straight away. Serve hot.

## Tips
- The softer the dough, the lighter the rings. Don't add flour to make it easier to handle: oil your hands instead.
- Make the hole wide: when frying, the dough puffs up and closes it.

## Common mistakes
- Don't fry too many rings at once: the oil cools and they turn greasy.
- Don't fry at too high a temperature: they darken outside before cooking inside.

## Local variations
- Some families make the dough without potatoes, with just flour, water and yeast.
- In the past they were also filled with rosamarina (sardella), which today is made only with the home version in recipe 15.
- They are also made sweet, rolled in sugar straight after frying.

## Storage
Best freshly made. Leftovers keep for 1 day in a paper bag and can be reheated in the oven at 180 °C (355 °F) for 5 minutes.

## Substitutions
Caciocavallo can be replaced by mild provolone. Anchovies in oil are sold everywhere.

## Allergens (14 EU allergens)
Gluten (wheat flour). Fish, if filled with anchovies. Milk, if filled with caciocavallo.
