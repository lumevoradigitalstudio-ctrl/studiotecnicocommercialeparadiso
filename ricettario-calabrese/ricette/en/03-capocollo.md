# Capocollo

*Capocollo · capicollu · all of Calabria · winter*

Capocollo comes from the top of the pig's back, between the neck and the loin (in other regions it is called *coppa*). It is a whole piece of meat, marbled with fat, that is neither ground nor mixed. That makes it the simplest cured meat to make at home, even if it is the longest to wait for. It is salted, washed with wine vinegar, coated in black pepper, wrapped and tied tightly. Then it rests for more than three months. Thinly sliced, it shows pink meat and white marbling. This recipe takes its curing time from the PDO specification. For the salt it uses the weight method, which is more precise than salting by eye.

**Makes:** 1 capocollo, about 1.3 kg (2.9 lb) · **Prep:** 1 hour · **Rest:** 12-14 days + at least 100 days · **Difficulty:** challenging

Before you start, read the **safety rules** at the beginning of this chapter.

## Ingredients

- 1 whole pork neck (capocollo or coppa), about 2 kg (4.4 lb), trimmed by the butcher
- total salt equal to 2.8% of the meat's weight, that is 56 g (2 oz) per 2 kg (4.4 lb), including the curing salt calculated as at the start of the chapter, dose 65 mg/kg
- 3 g ground black pepper, for salting
- 250 ml (1 cup) red wine vinegar
- 30 g (1 oz) coarsely ground black pepper, for the coating
- 10 g (0.4 oz) ground Calabrian chilli, sweet or hot, optional
- 1 wide natural casing (beef bung) or a sheet of pork caul fat
- butcher's twine
- 1 large zip-lock food bag, or a vacuum bag

## Method

1. Weigh the capocollo and write down the weight. Calculate the salt and curing salt on that weight.
2. Mix the salt, curing salt and the 3 g of pepper.
3. Rub the mixture into the meat on every side, working it into the folds.
4. Put the capocollo in the bag with any leftover salt, squeeze out the air and seal.
5. Leave it in the fridge at 2-4 °C (36-39 °F) for 12-14 days, about one day per centimetre of thickness plus two. Turn it every day and massage it through the bag.
6. At the end of salting the meat is firmer and darker. Take it out of the bag and rinse it quickly under cold water.
7. Rub it well with the vinegar and pat it dry with paper towels.
8. Coat it with the coarse pepper (and the chilli, if using), pressing with your hands.
9. Stuff it into the casing, or wrap it tightly in the caul fat. Prick the casing with a needle to let air out.
10. Tie it tightly with twine in a spiral, one turn every 3 cm (1¼ in), and finish with a loop to hang it.
11. Weigh it and write the weight on a tag.
12. Hang it at 12-15 °C (54-59 °F) with 75-85% humidity, in a dark, ventilated place.
13. Weigh it every two weeks. It is ready when it has lost 30-35% of its weight and after **at least 100 days**, as the PDO specification requires.
14. Cut the first slice from the middle. It should be dark pink and supple, with no grey or soft areas.

## Tips
- Ask the butcher for an evenly shaped neck: it dries more evenly.
- After the first month, if the surface becomes hard, raise the humidity and reduce the airflow.

## Common mistakes
- Don't shorten the salting: the salt has to reach the centre.
- Don't cut it before 100 days: it looks ready outside, but inside it isn't.

## Local variations
- In some areas capocollo is buried in coarse salt for a number of days decided by experience. That is the historical method. The weight method gives the same result with fewer mistakes.
- The coating can be black pepper only, or pepper and chilli.

## Storage
Whole: hung in a cool place or in the fridge, wrapped in paper, for 4-6 months. It can also be vacuum-packed. Once cut, cover the cut end with cling film and eat within 3-4 weeks.

## Substitutions
Capocollo is the same cut as Italian *coppa* or pork collar. Beef bungs are sold by butchers; otherwise use meat-curing cloth.

## Allergens (14 EU allergens)
Sulphites, possible in wine vinegar.

## PDO and PGI
**Capocollo di Calabria PDO** is a certified product. Home-made capocollo cannot use this name.

## Wine
A **Cirò Rosso DOC**.
