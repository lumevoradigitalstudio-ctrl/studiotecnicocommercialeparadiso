# Pitta catanzarese (Catanzaro ring bread)

*Pitta catanzarese · Catanzaro · all year*

Pitta is the bread for morzello. In Catanzaro it is a low ring with a wide hole in the middle, a crisp crust and little crumb. It is cut into quarters and each quarter is opened like a little book, ready to be filled with hot morzello (recipe 39) and eaten with your hands, no cutlery. But pitta is also good with cured meats, with 'nduja or with peppers and potatoes. This recipe makes four, enough for a morzello for four people. The secret of the shape is the hole: stretch it well before the last rise, because in the oven the dough grows and tends to close it.

**Makes:** 4 pitte · **Prep:** 30 minutes · **Cook:** 20 minutes · **Rest:** 2 hours 45 minutes · **Total:** about 3 hours 30 minutes · **Difficulty:** medium

## Ingredients

- 300 g (11 oz) Italian type 0 flour, or strong white bread flour
- 200 g (7 oz) semola rimacinata (re-milled durum wheat semolina)
- 320 ml (1¼ cups) lukewarm water, about 30 °C (86 °F)
- 5 g (0.2 oz) fresh yeast, or 2 g dried yeast
- 10 g (0.4 oz) fine salt

**Equipment:** two baking trays lined with baking paper, tea towels.

## Method

1. Dissolve the yeast in the lukewarm water.
2. Mix the flour and semolina in a bowl and pour in the water a little at a time, kneading.
3. Add the salt and knead on the work surface for 10 minutes, until the dough is smooth and elastic.
4. Cover and leave to rise for 2 hours in a warm place, until doubled.
5. Divide the dough into 4 pieces of about 200 g (7 oz) and shape them into balls. Cover them and let them rest for 15 minutes.
6. Flatten each ball with your hands into a disc 2 cm (¾ in) thick and 15 cm (6 in) wide.
7. Push your fingers through the middle of the disc down to the surface and open a hole.
8. Stretch the hole with your fingers, turning the dough like a steering wheel, to 8-10 cm (3¼-4 in) across. The ring should be low and wide.
9. Put the pitte on the trays, well apart, and cover them. Let them rise for 30 minutes.
10. Heat the oven to 240 °C (465 °F), conventional (no fan).
11. Before baking, stretch the hole again with your fingers if it has narrowed.
12. Bake for 18-20 minutes, until golden and hollow-sounding when tapped underneath.
13. Let them cool slightly on a rack.
14. To serve with morzello, cut them into quarters and open each quarter in half, like a little book.

## Tips
- Pitte are best eaten the same day, still warm.
- For a crisper crust, put a small pan of water on the bottom of the oven for the first 10 minutes.

## Common mistakes
- Don't leave the hole narrow: in the oven it closes and the pitta turns into a bread roll.
- Don't make them too tall: pitta catanzarese is low, with more crust than crumb.

## Local variations
- In other parts of Calabria pitta has a narrower hole and more crumb.

## Storage
In a paper bag for 1-2 days. They can be frozen and reheated in the oven at 200 °C (390 °F) for 5 minutes.

## Substitutions
If you can't find semolina, use 500 g (1.1 lb) of strong white flour and 300 ml (1¼ cups) of water.

## Allergens (14 EU allergens)
Gluten (wheat flour, durum wheat semolina).

## PDO and PGI
None.

## Wine
Not applicable (see morzello, recipe 39).
