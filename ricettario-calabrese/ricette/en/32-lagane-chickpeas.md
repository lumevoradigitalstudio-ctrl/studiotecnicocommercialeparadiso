# Lagane with chickpeas

*Lagane e ceci · lagane e ciciari · Catanzaro, common across the region · Saint Joseph's Day*

Lagane are wide strips of hand-rolled pasta, and *ciciari* are chickpeas. Together they make one of the oldest and humblest dishes in Calabria. Around Catanzaro, on Saint Joseph's Day, 19 March, a table was laid for the village poor, *u cumbitu*, and lagane with chickpeas was the first course. Every village has its own version. Some make it plain, with garlic, oil and chilli, and some add tomato or pancetta. This recipe is the plain one, the simplest. Dried chickpeas must be soaked the night before. The lagane are rolled out with a rolling pin in a few minutes.

**Serves:** 4 · **Prep:** 45 minutes · **Cook:** 2 hours 30 minutes · **Rest:** 12 hours + 30 minutes · **Difficulty:** medium

## Ingredients

**For the chickpeas**
- 300 g (11 oz) dried chickpeas
- 2 bay leaves
- 1 garlic clove
- salt

**For the lagane**
- 300 g (11 oz) semola rimacinata (re-milled durum wheat semolina)
- about 150 ml (¾ cup) warm water
- 1 pinch salt

**For the dressing**
- 60 ml (¼ cup) extra virgin olive oil, plus a drizzle for serving
- 2 garlic cloves, sliced
- 1 dried chilli, crumbled
- black pepper

## Method

**The chickpeas**
1. The night before, rinse the chickpeas and soak them in plenty of cold water for 12 hours.
2. Drain and rinse them, and put them in a pot with 2 l (2 quarts) of cold water, the bay and the garlic.
3. Bring to the boil, skim and cook over low heat, covered, for 2-2½ hours, until the chickpeas crush easily between your fingers. Add salt only in the last 15 minutes.
4. Keep the chickpeas in their cooking water.

**The lagane**
5. Knead the semolina, water and salt for 10 minutes, until the dough is smooth and firm. Cover and leave to rest for 30 minutes.
6. Divide the dough in two and roll out each half with a rolling pin on a surface dusted with semolina, to a thickness of 2 mm (⅛ in).
7. Let the sheet dry for 10 minutes, then roll it up loosely.
8. Cut the roll into strips 2 cm (¾ in) wide. Unroll them and cut them 10-12 cm (4-4¾ in) long.
9. Spread them out on a tray with semolina.

**The dish**
10. In a large pan heat the oil with the garlic and chilli over low heat for 2 minutes, until the garlic is golden.
11. Add the drained chickpeas and 2 ladles of their water. Cook for 5 minutes and crush a few chickpeas with a fork, to thicken.
12. Cook the lagane in boiling salted water for 3-4 minutes, until tender.
13. Lift them out with a slotted spoon and add them to the pan with the chickpeas.
14. Stir for 1 minute, adding chickpea water if needed. The dish should be soft, neither dry nor soupy.
15. Serve with a drizzle of raw olive oil and a grind of pepper.

## Tips
- The chickpea cooking water is precious: keep it all, it adjusts the creaminess.
- Chickpeas cooked the day before make the dish quicker.

## Common mistakes
- Don't salt the chickpeas at the start: they stay hard.
- Don't roll the dough too thin: the lagane break among the chickpeas.

## Local variations
- With a ripe tomato or a spoonful of tomato sauce in the base.
- With diced pancetta (recipe 04) browned in the oil.
- With tinned cooked chickpeas, to save time.

## Storage
Cooked chickpeas keep in the fridge in their water for 3 days, or in the freezer for 3 months. The finished dish is best freshly made.

## Substitutions
Instead of lagane, wide egg tagliatelle or pappardelle work, or broken short pasta.

## Allergens (14 EU allergens)
Gluten (durum wheat semolina).

## PDO and PGI
Optional: a **Calabrian PDO** extra virgin olive oil or **Olio di Calabria PGI**.

## Wine
A **Lamezia DOC red**.
