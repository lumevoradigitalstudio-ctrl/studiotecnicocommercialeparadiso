# Mushrooms in oil

*Funghi sott'olio · Sila, province of Cosenza · autumn*

In autumn the forests of the Sila plateau fill with mushroom hunters, and in many mountain homes the surplus ends up under oil for the winter. It makes a rich antipasto, served with bread and cured meats. It is also one of the most delicate home preserves. Mushrooms are not acidic, grow in contact with the soil and can carry botulism spores, and in Italy there have been cases of botulism linked to home-made mushrooms in oil. This recipe follows the cautious rules for preserves: inspected mushrooms, cooked in vinegar, dried flavourings only, and processed jars.

**Makes:** 4 jars of 250 ml (1 cup) · **Prep:** 1 hour · **Cook:** 30 minutes · **Rest:** 2-3 hours + 10-15 days · **Difficulty:** medium

## Before you start: safety

- **Inspected mushrooms.** Use only mushrooms bought from a shop or, if you forage them, have them checked by an expert (in Italy, the free mycology service of the local health authority, ASL). The wrong mushroom can kill.
- **Acidity.** Mushrooms must be cooked in water and vinegar. Oil alone does not protect them.
- **Processing and new lids**, as for the other vegetables in oil (recipe 09).

## Ingredients

- 1.5 kg (3.3 lb) firm, healthy mushrooms (small porcini, honey mushrooms, button mushrooms or mixed), already cleaned
- 750 ml (3¼ cups) white wine vinegar with at least 6% acidity
- 500 ml (2 cups) water
- 15 g (0.5 oz) fine salt
- 2 dried bay leaves
- 1 teaspoon black peppercorns
- 1 teaspoon dried chilli flakes
- about 500 ml (2 cups) extra virgin olive oil

**Equipment:** brush or cloth to clean the mushrooms, slotted spoon, clean tea towel, 250 ml (1 cup) jars with new lids, tall pot for processing.

## Method

1. Clean the mushrooms with a damp cloth or a brush, removing soil and damaged parts. Trim the base of the stems.
2. Leave small ones whole. Cut large ones into 2-3 cm (¾-1¼ in) pieces.
3. Bring the vinegar, water and salt to the boil in a stainless-steel pot.
4. Add the mushrooms and, from when the boil returns, cook for 5 minutes (7-8 minutes for porcini). They should be tender but firm.
5. Drain them and discard the cooking liquid.
6. Spread them on a clean tea towel, cover with another and let them dry for 2-3 hours, until dry to the touch.
7. Layer them in the jars with the bay, pepper and chilli, pressing down, up to 2 cm (¾ in) below the rim.
8. Cover with oil. Wait 30 minutes, press with a fork to release air, and top up to 1-2 cm (⅜-¾ in) below the rim.
9. Wipe the rims and close with the new lids.
10. Put the jars in the tall pot, covered by a few centimetres of cold water, separated by a tea towel.
11. Bring to a rolling boil and from that moment count 20 minutes. If the boil stops, start again from zero.
12. Let them cool in the water, then dry the jars and check that the lids are tightly sealed.
13. Store in a cool, dark place. Check the jars for 10-15 days: the oil must stay clear, with no bubbles.

## Tips
- Choose small, firm mushrooms: large spongy ones absorb oil and fall apart.
- Don't wash mushrooms under running water: they soak it up. Use a damp cloth.

## Common mistakes
- Don't use foraged mushrooms that haven't been inspected.
- Don't add fresh garlic, parsley or mint to the jar.
- Never taste a suspicious preserve.

## When to throw a jar away
Throw it away **without tasting** if the lid is bulging, if you see bubbles, cloudy oil or foam, or if there is a hiss of gas or a strange smell when you open it.

## Local variations
- Some homes add a clove or a few fennel seeds.
- Raw honey mushrooms are toxic: before any preparation, boil them in plenty of water and throw the water away. Always have foraged mushrooms checked by an expert (in Italy, the mycological service of the local health authority).

## Storage
- Closed, processed jars: in a cool, dark place, within 2 months.
- Opened jar: in the fridge, always covered with oil, within 7 days.

## Substitutions
Cultivated mushrooms such as button mushrooms or king oyster mushrooms work, as long as they are firm and fresh.

## Allergens (14 EU allergens)
Sulphites, possible in wine vinegar.

## PDO and PGI
Optional: a **Calabrian PDO** extra virgin olive oil or **Olio di Calabria PGI**.

## Wine
A **Terre di Cosenza DOC red**.
