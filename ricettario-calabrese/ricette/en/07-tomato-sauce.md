# Home-bottled tomato sauce

*Salsa di pomodoro di casa · 'a sarsa · all of Calabria · August*

In August, many Calabrian families set aside a day for the tomato sauce. Crates of ripe tomatoes are bought or picked, a fire is lit in the yard, and everyone has a job: washing, cutting, turning the handle of the food mill, filling the bottles. By the end of the day the larder is full of red, and the Sunday sauce is taken care of until next summer. The recipe is simple, but tomatoes are not always acidic enough for a safe preserve. So this version adds a little lemon juice, and the bottles are processed in boiling water for the times given by the Italian National Institute of Health (ISS).

**Makes:** about 6 bottles of 500 ml (2 cups) · **Prep:** 1 hour · **Cook:** 1 hour 15 minutes · **Total:** about 3 hours · **Difficulty:** medium

## Before you start: safety

- Use only sound, ripe tomatoes, with no rotten or bruised parts.
- Always add acid: **30 ml (2 tbsp) bottled lemon juice or 2.5 g citric acid for every litre of sauce** (USDA guidance). Some Italian sources give lower doses: this book uses the more cautious one.
- Use undamaged bottles or jars and **new caps or lids** every time.

## Ingredients

- 5 kg (11 lb) ripe sauce tomatoes, such as San Marzano or other plum tomatoes
- 90 ml (½ cup) bottled lemon juice, or 7.5 g (0.3 oz) citric acid, for 3 l (3¼ quarts) of sauce
- 10 g (0.4 oz) fine salt, optional
- 1 bunch fresh basil, washed and dried

**Equipment:** large pot, food mill, funnel, 6 bottles of 500 ml (2 cups) with new caps, tall pot for processing, tea towels.

## Method

1. Wash the tomatoes in cold water, remove the stems and discard any with spots or soft parts.
2. Cut the tomatoes in half lengthwise.
3. Put them in the large pot, without water, over medium heat. Cover and cook for 15 minutes, stirring now and then, until they soften and release their juice.
4. Pour off the clear water that collects at the bottom, if there is a lot. The sauce will be thicker.
5. Pass the hot tomatoes through the food mill into a clean pot. Discard skins and seeds.
6. Bring the sauce to the boil and cook over medium heat for 20 minutes, stirring, until it thickens a little.
7. Measure the sauce with a jug to know how many litres you have, then add the lemon juice (30 ml per litre) and the salt. Stir well.
8. Put a basil leaf in each clean bottle.
9. Fill the bottles using the funnel, leaving 2-3 cm (¾-1¼ in) of headspace below the cap.
10. Wipe the necks with a clean cloth and close with the new caps.
11. Stand the bottles upright in the tall pot, wrapped in tea towels so they don't knock together.
12. Cover them with cold water: it must come at least 5 cm (2 in) above the caps.
13. Bring to a rolling boil and from that moment count **35 minutes for 500 ml (2 cups) bottles** (40 minutes for 1 litre bottles). If the boil stops, start counting again from zero.
14. Turn off the heat and leave the bottles to cool in the water until the next day.
15. Dry the bottles and check the caps: they must be tightly closed and, if they are lids, curved slightly downwards.
16. Store in a cool, dark place. For 10-15 days, check that there are no bubbles, foam or bulging caps.

## Tips
- Wash and dry the basil well: water on the leaves carries soil and germs.
- For a thicker sauce, cook it 10 minutes longer at step 6, before adding the lemon.
- The lemon juice can't be tasted once the sauce is cooked with olive oil and onion.

## Common mistakes
- Don't skip the acid: even very ripe tomatoes may not be acidic enough.
- Don't fill the bottles to the brim: the sauce expands during processing and the cap pops off.
- Don't use bottles with chipped necks.

## When to throw a bottle away
Throw it away **without tasting** if the cap is bulging, if you see bubbles or foam, or if there is a hiss of gas or a strange smell when you open it.

## Local variations
- In many homes the tomatoes are blanched whole before milling, instead of cooked in pieces.

## Storage
- Closed bottles: in a cool, dark place within a year.
- Opened bottle: in the fridge, use within 4-5 days.

## Substitutions
Any meaty plum tomato with few seeds works, such as Roma. Bottled lemon juice has a consistent acidity, fresh lemon juice does not: that is why it is preferred.
