# Frese (twice-baked bread rings) with tomato and oregano

*Frese, friselle · all of Calabria · summer*

Frese are bread rings baked twice: first whole, then split in half, until they are dry and hard like rusks. To eat them, you pass them briefly under cold water and top them with ripe tomato, oregano, olive oil and salt. It is the summer lunch par excellence, with no need to turn on the stove. This page shows how to make them at home, and how to dress them. If you buy them ready-made, skip to step 13. Home-made, in a tin, they keep for weeks.

**Makes:** 12 half-frese · **Prep:** 30 minutes · **Cook:** 1 hour 30 minutes · **Rest:** 2 hours 30 minutes · **Total:** about 5 hours · **Difficulty:** medium

## Ingredients

**For the frese**
- 350 g (12 oz) semola rimacinata (re-milled durum wheat semolina)
- 150 g (5½ oz) Italian type 0 flour, or strong white bread flour
- 300 ml (1¼ cups) lukewarm water, about 30 °C (86 °F)
- 5 g (0.2 oz) fresh yeast, or 2 g dried yeast
- 10 g (0.4 oz) fine salt

**To dress 4 half-frese**
- 2-3 ripe tomatoes, about 300 g (11 oz)
- 4 tablespoons extra virgin olive oil
- dried oregano, salt
- optional: thinly sliced Tropea red onion, chilli, olives

## Method

**The frese**
1. Dissolve the yeast in the lukewarm water.
2. Mix the semolina and flour, pour in the water and knead. Add the salt and work for 10 minutes, until the dough is smooth.
3. Cover and leave to rise for 1 hour 30 minutes, until doubled.
4. Divide the dough into 6 pieces of about 135 g (5 oz).
5. Roll each piece into a rope 30 cm (12 in) long and close it into a ring, overlapping and pressing the ends together.
6. Put the rings on the tray lined with baking paper, cover and let them rise for 1 hour.
7. Heat the oven to 200 °C (390 °F) and bake for 20-25 minutes, until lightly golden.
8. Take them out and let them cool for 10 minutes.
9. With a serrated knife, cut each ring in half horizontally, like a bread roll. Or, as was done in the past, split them with a taut piece of string.
10. Lower the oven to 150 °C (300 °F).
11. Put the half-rings back in the oven cut side up and bake them for 50-60 minutes, until dry, hard and golden right through.
12. Let them cool in the turned-off oven, with the door ajar.

**The topping**
13. Dice the tomatoes and put them in a bowl with the oil, oregano and a pinch of salt. Leave for 10 minutes.
14. Pass each half-fresa under cold water for 2-3 seconds, or dip it in and take it out straight away. It should soften outside and stay crunchy inside.
15. Put them on a plate and leave them for 2 minutes.
16. Cover them with the tomatoes and their juice, and add onion, chilli or olives, if you like.

## Tips
- Splitting with string gives a rougher surface, which soaks up the dressing better.
- Don't wet them too much: they fall apart. Better too little, and let the tomato do the rest.

## Common mistakes
- Don't shorten the second bake: if they stay moist in the middle, they go mouldy in the jar.
- Don't put them in a jar while still warm.

## Local variations
- With tuna in oil and Tropea onion.
- With a teaspoon of 'nduja melted into the dressing oil.
- With home-made ricotta (recipe 19) and olives.

## Storage
Completely dry frese: in a tin or a paper bag, in a dry place, for 1 month.

## Substitutions
Frese are also sold ready-made in shops selling southern Italian products. For the topping you need ripe, tasty tomatoes.

## Allergens (14 EU allergens)
Gluten (durum wheat semolina, wheat flour).
