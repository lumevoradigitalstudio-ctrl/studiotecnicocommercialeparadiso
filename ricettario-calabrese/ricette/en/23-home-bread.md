# Durum wheat home bread

*Pane di casa di grano duro · all of Calabria · all year*

In the past, in many Calabrian villages, bread was kneaded at home and baked in a wood-fired oven. The wheat was almost always durum wheat, finely milled, and it gave a bread with a yellow, fragrant crumb that stays good for days. This recipe adapts that bread to a home oven. It uses *semola rimacinata*, finely re-milled durum wheat semolina, and fresh yeast, which is easier to handle than sourdough, and plenty of water for a soft crumb. It is the base bread for many recipes in this book: bread balls, pasta with breadcrumbs, licurdia. And, of course, for mopping up sauce.

**Makes:** 2 loaves of about 800 g (1.8 lb) · **Prep:** 30 minutes · **Cook:** 50 minutes · **Rest:** 3-4 hours · **Total:** about 5 hours · **Difficulty:** medium

## Ingredients

- 1 kg (2.2 lb) semola rimacinata (finely re-milled durum wheat semolina)
- 700 ml (3 cups) lukewarm water, about 30 °C (86 °F)
- 7 g (0.2 oz) fresh yeast, or 3 g dried yeast
- 20 g (0.7 oz) fine salt
- 1 tablespoon extra virgin olive oil, optional

**Equipment:** large bowl, tea towels, baking tray or baking stone, a small pan of water for steam.

## Method

1. Dissolve the yeast in 200 ml (¾ cup) of the lukewarm water.
2. Put the semolina in a large bowl and pour in the water with the yeast. Stir with a spoon.
3. Add the rest of the water a little at a time, kneading by hand, until no dry semolina is left.
4. Leave it covered for 15 minutes: the semolina absorbs the water and the dough becomes easier to work.
5. Add the salt and oil and knead on the work surface for 10-12 minutes, until the dough is smooth, elastic and slightly sticky.
6. Put it back in the oiled bowl, cover with a tea towel and leave it to rise in a warm place, at 24-26 °C (75-79 °F), for 2-3 hours, until doubled.
7. Turn it out onto a surface dusted with semolina and divide it in two.
8. Shape two loaves: fold the edges into the centre, turn the dough over and rotate it on the surface with cupped hands, until the surface is taut.
9. Put the loaves on a tray lined with baking paper, dust them with semolina and cover them. Let them rise for 1 hour, until puffy and, when pressed with a finger, the dent springs back slowly.
10. Heat the oven to 240 °C (465 °F) with a small pan of water on the bottom for steam.
11. Slash the top of each loaf with a sharp blade.
12. Bake for 15 minutes at 240 °C (465 °F).
13. Remove the pan of water, lower to 200 °C (390 °F) and bake for another 30-35 minutes.
14. The bread is done when the crust is deep golden and the loaf sounds hollow when tapped underneath.
15. Let it cool on a rack for at least 1 hour before slicing.

## Tips
- With sourdough: use 250 g (9 oz) refreshed sourdough starter instead of the yeast, use 100 ml (½ cup) less water and extend the first rise to 5-6 hours.
- Semolina absorbs a lot of water: at first the dough seems too soft, then it firms up as you knead.

## Common mistakes
- Don't slice the bread while hot: the crumb is still moist and turns gummy.
- Don't add too much semolina to the surface while kneading: the bread comes out dry.

## Local variations
- In many homes a single large round or ring-shaped loaf is made.
- In the cooling wood-fired oven, after the bread, came the olives (recipe 13) or biscuits `[TO BE VERIFIED]`.

## Storage
In a paper or cloth bag, for 3-4 days. It can be frozen in slices.

## Substitutions
Semola rimacinata is sold in Italian shops and many supermarkets; it is sometimes labelled "durum wheat semolina, re-milled". If you only have strong white bread flour, use it with 650 ml (2¾ cups) of water.
