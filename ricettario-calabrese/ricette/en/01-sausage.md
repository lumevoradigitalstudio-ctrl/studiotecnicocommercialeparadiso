# Fresh and dry sausage

*Salsiccia fresca e secca · sazizza · all of Calabria · winter*

Sausage is the first thing made from the pig, and the easiest cured meat to learn. It is made from pork shoulder and fat, chopped or coarsely ground, with salt, black pepper and Calabrian chilli, sweet or hot. Many families tie it into a chain or a horseshoe. Fresh, it goes on the grill or into a pan with potatoes. Cured, it becomes *salsiccia secca*, dry sausage to slice and eat with bread. This page gives you both: they start the same way, then go their separate ways. Fresh sausage is something anyone can make. Dry sausage needs the same care as any cured meat.

**Makes:** about 2 kg (4.4 lb) fresh or 1.4 kg (3.1 lb) dry · **Prep:** 1 hour 30 minutes · **Rest:** 12 hours (fresh), 48 hours + 3-5 weeks (dry) · **Difficulty:** medium / challenging

Before you start, read the **safety rules** at the beginning of this chapter.

## Ingredients

**For 2 kg (4.4 lb) of meat**
- 1.5 kg (3.3 lb) pork shoulder with its fat
- 500 g (1.1 lb) fresh skinless pork belly, or jowl fat
- 50 g (2 oz) salt in total, that is 2.5%. For dry sausage, part of it is curing salt (see step 4).
- 4 g coarsely ground black pepper
- 20-30 g (0.7-1 oz) ground Calabrian chilli, sweet or hot, to taste
- 6 g (0.2 oz) fennel seeds, optional (many families use them) `[TO BE VERIFIED: how common by area]`
- 60 ml (¼ cup) cold red wine, optional
- **dry sausage only:** 0.5 g starter culture for cured sausages, or the dose on the pack
- 4-5 m (13-16 ft) natural hog casings, 32-36 mm (1¼-1½ in) wide

## Method

**The base, for both**
1. Put the meat and belly in the freezer for 30-40 minutes, until firm at the edges but not frozen.
2. Cut everything into 3 cm (1¼ in) cubes, removing sinews and cartilage.
3. Soak the casings in lukewarm water for 30 minutes and rinse them inside too.
4. Weigh the salt and spices. **For dry sausage**, calculate the curing salt as explained at the start of the chapter (limit 80 mg/kg) and bring the total to 50 g (2 oz) with sea salt. Dissolve the culture in a tablespoon of cold, unchlorinated water.
5. Grind with the 8-10 mm (⅜ in) plate. Or chop the meat by hand with a knife into half-centimetre pieces.
6. Add salt, pepper, chilli, fennel and the wine if using (and the culture, for dry sausage).
7. Knead with your hands for 3-4 minutes, until the mixture turns sticky and holds together.
8. Fry a teaspoon of the mixture and taste it. Adjust only the spices, never the curing salt.
9. Slide the casing onto the stuffer nozzle, leaving 10 cm (4 in) free, and tie a knot.
10. Stuff without packing too hard, guiding the casing with one hand. Prick any air bubbles with a needle.
11. Every 12-15 cm (4¾-6 in), twist the sausage twice, alternating the direction, or tie it with string to make a chain.

**Fresh sausage**
12. Lay it on a tray, cover and leave it in the fridge for 12 hours, so the flavours come together.
13. Eat within 3 days. Always cook it right through, with no pink in the middle (at least 71 °C (160 °F) at the centre).

**Dry sausage**
14. Hang it for 48 hours at 20-24 °C (68-75 °F) with humidity above 90%.
15. Measure the pH: it must be 5.3 or lower `[TO BE VERIFIED: threshold]`. If it has not dropped within 72 hours, throw it away.
16. Cure at 12-15 °C (54-59 °F) with 75-85% humidity. Halfway through you can press it lightly between two boards to flatten it.
17. It is ready when it has lost 30% of its weight, usually in 3-5 weeks. Inside it must be firm and evenly red.

## Tips
- The right amount of fat is about a quarter of the weight: with less, the sausage is dry and stringy.
- Don't overwork the mixture with warm hands: the fat melts and the sausage turns crumbly.

## Common mistakes
- Don't overfill the casing: the sausage bursts when you twist it or cook it.
- Don't cure fresh sausage made without curing salt and culture: it is only for cooking and eating.

## Local variations
- Around Cosenza hot sausage with plenty of chilli is common; elsewhere the sweet one `[TO BE VERIFIED]`.
- Some families add pieces of liver (liver sausage), to be eaten only cooked `[TO BE VERIFIED: how common in Calabria]`.

## Storage
- Fresh: 3 days in the fridge, 3 months in the freezer.
- Dry: hung in a cool place or in the fridge, wrapped in paper, for 2-3 months `[TO BE VERIFIED]`. It can also be vacuum-packed.

## Substitutions
Natural casings are sold by butchers. If you can't find ground Calabrian chilli, mix sweet (unsmoked) paprika and cayenne pepper.

## Allergens (14 EU allergens)
None in the basic ingredients. If you use wine, it may contain sulphites.

## PDO and PGI
**Salsiccia di Calabria PDO** is a certified product. Home-made sausage cannot use this name.

## Wine
With dry sausage and bread: a **Savuto DOC red**.
