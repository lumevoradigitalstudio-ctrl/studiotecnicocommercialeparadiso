# Flat and rolled pancetta

*Pancetta tesa e arrotolata · all of Calabria · winter*

Pancetta is the everyday cured meat. It goes into the base of sauces, it is browned with beans and lentils, and it is eaten in thin slices on warm bread. It comes from the pig's belly, in layers of fat and lean. It is salted, washed with wine vinegar and seasoned with pepper or chilli. Then it is either hung flat to dry, which is *pancetta tesa*, or rolled tightly and tied, which is *pancetta arrotolata*. The flat one is ready in a few weeks and is the best choice if you are curing meat for the first time. The rolled one takes more time and care, but it looks beautiful when sliced.

**Makes:** about 1.4 kg (3.1 lb) · **Prep:** 1 hour · **Rest:** 7-10 days + 5-10 weeks · **Difficulty:** medium

Before you start, read the **safety rules** at the beginning of this chapter.

## Ingredients

- 1 piece of fresh pork belly, about 2 kg (4.4 lb), 4-5 cm (1½-2 in) thick, with the skin (for flat pancetta) or without (for rolled)
- total salt equal to 2.8% of the meat's weight, that is 56 g (2 oz) per 2 kg (4.4 lb), including the curing salt calculated as at the start of the chapter, dose 65 mg/kg
- 3 g ground black pepper
- 2 bay leaves, crumbled, optional
- 200 ml (¾ cup) wine vinegar
- 20 g (0.7 oz) coarsely ground black pepper, or 30 g (1 oz) ground Calabrian chilli, sweet or hot, for the coating
- for rolled pancetta: butcher's twine and a wide casing or caul fat
- 1 large zip-lock food bag, or a vacuum bag

## Method

**Salting**
1. Weigh the belly and trim the edges into a neat rectangle. Calculate the salt and curing salt on the weight.
2. Mix salt, curing salt, pepper and bay. Rub it into the meat on every side.
3. Put it in the bag with any leftover salt, squeeze out the air and seal.
4. Leave it in the fridge at 2-4 °C (36-39 °F) for 7-10 days, turning it every day. It is ready when it is firm even in the thickest part. The PDO specification allows 4 to 11 days of dry salting.
5. Rinse it under cold water, rub it with the vinegar and dry it well.

**Flat pancetta**
6. Coat the meat side with the coarse pepper or the chilli, pressing with your hands.
7. Pierce one corner, thread the twine through and hang it flat.
8. Weigh it and cure it at 12-15 °C (54-59 °F) with 75-85% humidity.
9. It is ready when it has lost 25-30% of its weight, usually in 5-6 weeks. The PDO specification requires at least 30 days.

**Rolled pancetta**
6. Remove the skin, if the butcher hasn't already.
7. Coat the meat side with pepper or chilli.
8. Roll the belly up tightly from the long side, leaving no air pockets in the middle.
9. Stuff it into the wide casing, or wrap it in the caul fat, and prick it with a needle.
10. Tie it tightly with twine, one turn every 2-3 cm (¾-1¼ in).
11. Weigh it and cure it at 12-15 °C (54-59 °F) with 75-85% humidity.
12. It is ready when it has lost 30% of its weight, usually in 8-10 weeks. When cut, it should show a tight spiral with no hole in the middle.

## Tips
- For your first attempt, make flat pancetta: it is easier and more forgiving.
- Roll it tight: a gap in the middle fills with air, and air makes the meat darken and spoil.

## Common mistakes
- Don't use belly that is too thin: under 3 cm (1¼ in) it turns hard and salty.
- Don't skip the vinegar wash: it removes excess salt and cleans the surface.

## Local variations
- With a chilli coating, which turns the pancetta red.
- Some families add fennel seeds or a little garlic to the salting.

## Storage
Whole: hung in a cool place or in the fridge, wrapped in paper, for 2-3 months. It can also be vacuum-packed. Once cut, keep it in the fridge covered with cling film.

## Substitutions
Ask the butcher for pork belly of even thickness. If you can't find ground Calabrian chilli, mix sweet paprika and cayenne pepper.

## Allergens (14 EU allergens)
Sulphites, possible in wine vinegar.

## PDO and PGI
**Pancetta di Calabria PDO** is a certified product. Home-made pancetta cannot use this name.

## Wine
A red from the Lamezia area, such as a **Scavigna DOC red**.
