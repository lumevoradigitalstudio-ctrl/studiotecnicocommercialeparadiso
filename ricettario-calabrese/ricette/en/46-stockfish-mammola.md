# Stockfish alla mammolese

*Stocco alla mammolese · Reggio Calabria, Mammola · all year*

Mammola is a village inland in the Locride, far from the sea. Yet its most famous dish is fish: *stocco*, cod dried in the open air that comes from far away. Stockfish is soaked for days in running water until it softens again, then cooked in an earthenware pot, the *tiana*, with potatoes, tomato, onion, olives and dried peppers, in plenty of olive oil. The result is a tasty, fragrant stew in which the fish stays in large pieces and the potatoes soak up the sauce. Pre-soaked stockfish is sold in many Calabrian fishmongers: it saves a week of work.

**Serves:** 4 · **Prep:** 20 minutes · **Cook:** 50 minutes · **Total:** 1 hour 10 minutes (with pre-soaked stockfish) · **Difficulty:** medium

## Ingredients

- 1 kg (2.2 lb) pre-soaked stockfish, or 300-350 g (11-12 oz) dry stockfish
- 100 ml (½ cup) extra virgin olive oil
- 1 red onion, thinly sliced
- 600 g (1.3 lb) yellow-fleshed potatoes
- 400 g (14 oz) tinned plum tomatoes or cherry tomatoes
- 100 g (3½ oz) black olives
- 2-3 dried sweet peppers, or 1 fresh red pepper
- 1 dried chilli, optional
- 1 handful parsley
- salt, if needed

**Equipment:** an earthenware casserole (with a heat diffuser) or a wide heavy-based pan.

## Method

**If the stockfish is dry**
1. Cut the dry stockfish into pieces with a kitchen saw, or ask the fishmonger to do it.
2. Soak it in cold water in the fridge for 5-7 days, changing the water twice a day, until soft and swollen `[TO BE VERIFIED: soaking time]`.

**The dish**
3. Remove the skin and the larger bones from the stockfish and cut it into 5-6 cm (2-2¼ in) pieces.
4. Peel the potatoes and cut them into wedges 2 cm (¾ in) thick.
5. If using dried peppers, remove the stems and seeds and break them into 3-4 pieces.
6. Heat the oil in the casserole over medium heat and cook the onion for 5 minutes, until soft.
7. Add the tomatoes, crushed by hand, and cook for 5 minutes.
8. Add the potatoes, stir and pour in 300 ml (1¼ cups) of hot water. Cook covered for 15 minutes.
9. Lay the stockfish on top of the potatoes, without stirring.
10. Add the olives, peppers and chilli.
11. Cover and cook over low heat for 20-25 minutes. Don't stir: shake the pan now and then, so the fish doesn't break.
12. The dish is ready when the potatoes are soft and the stockfish flakes into large pieces.
13. Taste the sauce and add salt only if needed. Sprinkle with parsley and serve in the pot.

## Tips
- Well-soaked stockfish is white, soft and without a strong smell.
- Earthenware holds the heat and cooks gently: if you use it, always put it on a heat diffuser.

## Common mistakes
- Don't stir the stockfish: it crumbles.
- Don't salt before tasting: stockfish can still be salty.

## Local variations
- Stockfish without tomato, with potatoes, olives and capers.
- Fried or grilled stockfish with oil, garlic and parsley.

## Storage
In the fridge for 2 days. Reheat over low heat, without stirring.

## Substitutions
Stockfish is sold at fishmongers and in Norwegian or Italian shops, sometimes pre-soaked. Don't confuse it with salt cod (baccalà), which is salted cod.

## Allergens (14 EU allergens)
Fish.

## PDO and PGI

## Wine
A light red from the Locride, such as a **Bivongi DOC red**.
