# Crushed green olives

*Olive schiacciate · alivi scacciati · Cosenza, common across the region · October-November*

When the olives are harvested in autumn, the first green ones don't go to the mill: they are crushed one by one with a smooth stone, until the flesh splits around the pit. Then they sit for days in fresh water, changed every day, until they lose their bitterness. Finally they are dressed with garlic, wild fennel, chilli and olive oil. They are crunchy, slightly bitter and fragrant, and go well with bread, cured meats and a glass of wine. This is a simple preserve, with no lye and no cooking, and one of the first things to learn at home.

**Makes:** about 1 kg (2.2 lb) · **Prep:** 1 hour · **Rest:** 8-12 days + 1 day · **Total:** about 2 weeks · **Difficulty:** easy

## Before you start: safety

Olives taken out of the water are not acidic enough to sit in oil at room temperature with raw garlic and herbs. This seasoned version is kept **in the fridge** and eaten within **2 weeks**. To keep them longer, use the brine described in the variations.

## Ingredients

- 1.2 kg (2.6 lb) fresh green olives, firm and unblemished
- fresh water for soaking
- 20 g (0.7 oz) fine salt
- 3 garlic cloves, sliced
- 2 tablespoons wild fennel seeds or flowers, or 1 tablespoon fennel seeds
- 1 fresh hot chilli, sliced, or 1 teaspoon dried chilli
- ½ sweet red pepper, in thin strips, optional
- 1 teaspoon dried oregano, optional
- 80 ml (¼ cup) extra virgin olive oil

**Equipment:** a clean smooth stone or a meat mallet, a chopping board, a large glass or food-grade plastic container.

## Method

1. Wash the olives and discard any with spots, holes or soft parts.
2. Put an olive on the board and give it one firm blow with the stone or mallet, until the flesh splits. The pit must stay inside, whole.
3. Put the crushed olives straight into fresh water, so they don't darken.
4. Cover the olives with water in the container and put a plate on top, so they stay submerged.
5. Change the water every day, in the morning. The water of the first days is dark and bitter.
6. From day 8, taste an olive. They are ready when they are still slightly bitter but pleasant, usually between day 8 and day 12.
7. Drain them and squeeze them by the handful to remove the water.
8. Season them in a bowl with salt, garlic, fennel, chilli, pepper and oregano.
9. Add the oil and mix well with clean hands.
10. Pack the olives into a clean container and cover.
11. Leave them in the fridge for 1 day to take on flavour, stirring once.

## Tips
- Olives picked only a few days before give the crunchiest result.
- Taste them every day after the first week: one more day in water removes bitterness but also texture.

## Common mistakes
- Don't crush too hard: the pit breaks and the olive falls apart.
- Don't leave seasoned olives out of the fridge for days.

## Local variations
- **In brine, for the winter:** after soaking, put the olives in clean jars and cover them with a brine of 100 g (3½ oz) salt per litre of boiled and cooled water, with 50 ml (3½ tbsp) wine vinegar per litre `[TO BE VERIFIED: safe amounts]`. Close and keep cool. When serving, rinse them and season as above.
- Some families add bay leaves or orange zest.

## Storage
- Seasoned olives: in the fridge, in a closed container, within 2 weeks.
- Olives in brine: in a cool place, for a few months `[TO BE VERIFIED]`.

## Substitutions
Fresh green olives are sold in autumn at markets and in Italian or Greek shops. If you can't find wild fennel, use fennel seeds.
