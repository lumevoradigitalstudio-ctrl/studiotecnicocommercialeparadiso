# Struncatura with anchovies and breadcrumbs: its history and a version for today

*Struncatura · Reggio Calabria, Gioia Tauro and the plain · all year*

Struncatura is a long, dark, rough pasta born on the plain of Gioia Tauro. For many years it wasn't sold in shops: it was traded almost secretly, because it was made with wholemeal flours and coarsely ground wheat, sometimes with what was left over from milling. That is why for a long time it was considered an outlaw pasta. Today struncatura is produced according to the rules, with wholemeal durum wheat flour, and restaurants serve it too. In Gioia Tauro the classic dressing is *ammollicato*: anchovies melted in olive oil and toasted breadcrumbs, with garlic and chilli. The alternative is 'nduja.

**Serves:** 4 · **Prep:** 15 minutes · **Cook:** 20 minutes · **Total:** 35 minutes · **Difficulty:** easy

## The story, in brief

Pasta made from milling waste did not meet the hygiene and trading rules for pasta. So it should not be made that way again: today's version uses wholemeal durum wheat flour, sold legally.

## Ingredients

- 400 g (14 oz) struncatura, or wholemeal durum wheat linguine
- 80 ml (¼ cup) extra virgin olive oil
- 2 garlic cloves, whole and crushed
- 8 anchovy fillets in oil, or 4 salted anchovies, cleaned and rinsed
- 1 dried chilli, crumbled
- 80 g (3 oz) stale bread, coarsely crumbled
- 2 tablespoons chopped parsley
- 1 tablespoon desalted capers, optional
- 50 g (2 oz) oven-dried black olives (recipe 13), chopped, optional

## Method

1. Bring 4 l (4¼ quarts) of water to the boil with 25 g (0.9 oz) of coarse salt: the anchovies are already salty.
2. In a small pan, toast the breadcrumbs in 2 tablespoons of the oil over medium heat for 4-5 minutes, stirring, until golden and crunchy. Set aside.
3. In a large pan, heat the rest of the oil with the garlic and chilli over low heat for 2 minutes, until the garlic is golden. Remove the garlic.
4. Take the pan off the heat and add the anchovies. Crush them with a wooden spoon until they melt into the oil.
5. Cook the pasta for the time on the packet, minus one minute. Keep back a cup of cooking water.
6. Drain the pasta into the pan with the anchovies. Add the capers and olives, if using, and a splash of cooking water.
7. Toss for 1 minute over high heat, until the pasta is glossy and well coated.
8. Turn off the heat, add the parsley and half the breadcrumbs and mix.
9. Serve straight away with the rest of the breadcrumbs on top.

## Tips
- Add the breadcrumbs only at the end, and partly on top: they must stay crunchy.
- Anchovies melt best off the heat, in oil that is hot but not sizzling.

## Common mistakes
- Don't burn the garlic: it makes the whole dish bitter.
- Don't add cheese: in this dish the breadcrumbs take its place.

## Local variations
- With 'nduja melted in the oil instead of anchovies, as served in some restaurants in Gioia Tauro.
- With fresh cherry tomatoes sautéed in the oil before the anchovies.

## Storage
Best freshly made. Toasted breadcrumbs keep for 3-4 days in a closed jar.

## Substitutions
Struncatura is sold online by Calabrian producers. Wholemeal durum wheat linguine or spaghetti work instead.

## Allergens (14 EU allergens)
Gluten (pasta, bread), fish (anchovies).

## PDO and PGI
Optional: a **Calabrian PDO** extra virgin olive oil or **Olio di Calabria PGI**.

## Wine
A white from the Ionian coast of Reggio, such as a **Bivongi DOC white**.
