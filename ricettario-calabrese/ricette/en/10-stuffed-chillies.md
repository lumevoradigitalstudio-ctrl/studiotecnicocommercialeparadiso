# Stuffed chillies in oil

*Peperoncini ripieni sott'olio · Vibo Valentia, common across the region · late summer*

These are little red balls, hot outside and soft inside, eaten in a single bite. They are made with round chillies, hollowed out, blanched in vinegar and stuffed with tuna, capers and anchovies, then covered with oil. On festive tables they appear next to the cured meats and cheeses, and they are always the first to go. The fish filling makes this preserve more delicate than the others, because tuna and anchovies are not acidic. So the recipe below is designed for the fridge and for eating within a few weeks. For a version to keep in the larder all year, I have not found a verified home method.

**Makes:** about 30 chillies · **Prep:** 1 hour · **Cook:** 5 minutes · **Rest:** 12 hours + 2 days · **Difficulty:** medium

## Before you start: safety

The fish filling is not acidic, and chillies under oil are an airless environment: together they carry a risk of botulism. This version is kept **always in the fridge** (0-4 °C (39 °F)) and eaten within **3 weeks**. Don't keep it in the larder.

## Ingredients

- 30 round hot chillies (cherry peppers), firm and glossy
- 500 ml (2 cups) white wine vinegar with at least 6% acidity
- 250 ml (1 cup) water
- 10 g (0.4 oz) fine salt
- 160 g (5½ oz) drained tuna in oil
- 30 g (1 oz) salted capers, desalted
- 6 anchovy fillets in oil
- about 300 ml (1¼ cups) extra virgin olive oil

**Equipment:** gloves, melon baller or small knife, clean 250 ml (1 cup) jars with new lids.

## Method

1. Put on gloves: chilli irritates skin and eyes.
2. Wash and dry the chillies. Cut a cap around the stem with a small knife.
3. Hollow them out with the melon baller or the handle of a teaspoon, removing seeds and membranes.
4. Bring the vinegar, water and salt to the boil.
5. Plunge the chillies into the boiling liquid for 3-4 minutes, until they soften a little but stay whole.
6. Drain them and turn them upside down on a clean tea towel. Let them dry for 12 hours in the fridge, covered with another tea towel.
7. Rinse and dry the capers. Chop the tuna, capers and anchovies finely with a knife or blender, to a soft paste.
8. Fill each chilli with a teaspoon of filling, pressing it in well.
9. Arrange them in the jars with the opening facing up, packed close together, up to 2 cm (¾ in) below the rim.
10. Cover with oil. Wait 30 minutes, top up to 1-2 cm (⅜-¾ in) below the rim and close.
11. Put the jars in the fridge straight away. They are ready after 2 days.

## Tips
- Choose chillies of the same size: they cook evenly.
- Take the jar out of the fridge 20 minutes before serving: cold oil sets.

## Common mistakes
- Don't keep the jar out of the fridge and don't use it after 3 weeks.
- Don't take the chillies out of the jar with your fingers: always use a clean fork.

## When to throw a jar away
Throw it away **without tasting** if you see bubbles, cloudy oil or foam, or notice a strange smell.

## Local variations
- Some add parsley or oregano to the filling.

## Storage
Only in the fridge, at 0-4 °C (32-39 °F), within 3 weeks. Once the jar is open, within 1 week.

## Substitutions
Round hot chillies are sold as "cherry peppers". If yours are not hot, add a pinch of ground chilli to the filling.

## Allergens (14 EU allergens)
Fish (tuna, anchovies). Sulphites, possible in wine vinegar.

## PDO and PGI
None.

## Wine
A fresh, savoury white, such as a **Cirò Bianco DOC**.
