# Fig honey (fig syrup)

*Miele di fichi · Cosenza · late summer*

Fig honey isn't made by bees. It is made by cooking ripe late-summer figs for a long time, squeezing out their juice and reducing it slowly over the heat, until it becomes a dark, thick, glossy syrup. It tastes of cooked figs and caramel. Around Cosenza it is used to glaze Christmas sweets such as turdilli (recipe 59), and it is poured over ricotta or cheese. Three kilos of figs give little more than one jar, which is why it is so precious. It takes time, and attention in the last minutes, when the syrup thickens quickly.

**Makes:** about 400 ml (1¾ cups) · **Prep:** 30 minutes · **Cook:** 3 hours 30 minutes · **Rest:** 1 night · **Difficulty:** medium

## Ingredients

- 3 kg (6.6 lb) very ripe fresh figs, even slightly split
- 1.5 l (6¼ cups) water

**Equipment:** large stainless-steel pot, potato masher or food mill, clean cotton cloth or cheesecloth, sieve, wide heavy-based pan, glass jars with new lids, kitchen thermometer (optional).

## Method

1. Wash the figs, remove the stems and discard any that are mouldy or sour.
2. Quarter the figs and put them in the pot with the water.
3. Bring to the boil and cook over medium heat for 1 hour, stirring now and then, until the figs have fallen apart.
4. Turn off the heat and let it cool a little.
5. Pour the figs into the cloth set in a sieve over a bowl. Close the cloth and squeeze hard, several times, to press out all the juice.
6. Leave the juice in the fridge overnight, so the pulp settles at the bottom.
7. Slowly pour the clear juice into the wide pan, leaving the sediment in the bowl.
8. Bring to the boil and reduce over medium-low heat for 2-2½ hours, skimming at first. The juice darkens and reduces more and more.
9. In the last 20-30 minutes stir often and lower the heat: now it thickens quickly.
10. Test the thickness: put a drop on a cold saucer. When after a minute it flows slowly like honey, it is ready (about 105-107 °C (220-225 °F), if using a thermometer).
11. Pour it boiling into hot, clean jars, up to 1 cm (⅜ in) below the rim, and close immediately with the new lids.
12. Turn the jars upside down for 5 minutes, then stand them upright and let them cool.

## Tips
- The riper the figs, the sweeter and darker the honey.
- It thickens further as it cools: take it off the heat when it is just a little runnier than bee honey.

## Common mistakes
- Don't overcook it: it turns into hard, bitter caramel.
- Don't leave it unattended at the end: it can stick and burn in a few minutes.

## Local variations
- Some families add orange or lemon zest while cooking the figs.
- In many areas it is also called "miele cotto" or "vincotto di fichi" `[TO BE VERIFIED: names by area]`.

## Storage
- Closed jars: in a cool, dark place for a few months `[TO BE VERIFIED: shelf life and safety]`.
- Opened jar: in the fridge, within 2 months. Always use a clean spoon.

## Substitutions
Any very ripe fresh figs, green or black, work. Otherwise use chestnut honey or grape vincotto for the Christmas sweets.

## Allergens (14 EU allergens)
None.

## PDO and PGI
**Fichi di Cosenza PDO** are dried figs. Home-made fig honey is not a PDO product.

## Wine
Not applicable: this is a preserve.
