# Sweet and sour tuna with onions

*Tonno in agrodolce con le cipolle · Pizzo and the Costa degli Dei, Vibo Valentia · spring-summer*

In Pizzo, on the coast near Vibo Valentia, tuna was for a long time the town's work and food. The tuna traps of Pizzo were in use until the early 1960s. One of the best-loved ways to cook it, on this coast and all over Calabria, is sweet and sour. The steaks are seared in a pan and covered with a mountain of Tropea red onions, stewed with vinegar and sugar until soft and glossy. It is eaten warm or cold, and the next day it is even better. Some local cooks scent it with a pinch of mint, while other recipes use thyme, raisins or pine nuts.

**Serves:** 4 · **Prep:** 15 minutes · **Cook:** 30 minutes · **Rest:** 30 minutes (optional) · **Difficulty:** easy

## Ingredients

- 4 fresh tuna steaks, 2 cm (¾ in) thick, about 160 g (5½ oz) each
- 600 g (1.3 lb) Tropea red onions
- 6 tablespoons extra virgin olive oil
- 80 ml (¼ cup) white wine vinegar
- 1 tablespoon sugar, about 15 g (0.5 oz)
- 2 tablespoons Italian 00 flour, or plain/all-purpose flour
- a few fresh mint leaves, or a sprig of thyme
- salt and pepper

## Method

1. Peel the onions and slice them thinly, 3-4 mm (⅛ in) thick.
2. In a wide frying pan heat 4 tablespoons of the oil over low heat. Add the onions with a pinch of salt.
3. Cook the onions covered for 15 minutes, stirring now and then, until soft and translucent.
4. Add the vinegar and sugar, stir and cook uncovered for 5 minutes, until the vinegar reduces and the onions are glossy. Set aside.
5. Dry the steaks, salt them and dust them with flour, shaking off the excess.
6. In another pan heat the rest of the oil over medium-high heat.
7. Sear the tuna for 2 minutes per side: it should stay pink in the middle.
8. Put the steaks in the pan with the onions and cover them with the onions.
9. Cook for 2 minutes over low heat, turning them once.
10. Add the torn mint leaves and a grind of pepper.
11. Serve warm. If you have time, leave it to rest for 30 minutes: the tuna takes on the sweet and sour flavour.

## Tips
- Fresh tuna is bright red and glossy. If it is dark or brown, it isn't fresh.
- Taste the sweet and sour onions before adding the tuna: if too sharp, add a pinch of sugar.

## Common mistakes
- Don't cook the tuna for long: it turns grey and dry.
- Don't burn the onions: they must stew gently.

## Local variations
- With raisins and pine nuts in the onions.
- With thyme on the tuna instead of mint.

## Storage
In the fridge, covered with the onions, for 2 days. It is good cold too.

## Substitutions
Yellowfin or bluefin tuna, fresh or thawed, both work. Instead of Tropea onions, a sweet red onion.

## Allergens (14 EU allergens)
Fish. Gluten (flour). Sulphites (wine vinegar).
