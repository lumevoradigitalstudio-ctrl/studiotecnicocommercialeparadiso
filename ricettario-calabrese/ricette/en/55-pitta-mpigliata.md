# Pitta 'mpigliata (Christmas pastry roses with walnuts and honey)

*Pitta 'mpigliata, pitta 'nchiusa · Cosenza, San Giovanni in Fiore · Christmas*

Pitta 'mpigliata is the most spectacular Christmas sweet in Calabria. Thin strips of pastry made with wine and olive oil are filled with walnuts, raisins, honey, orange zest and cinnamon, rolled into little roses and packed tightly into a round tin, like a crown. In the oven they turn golden and crisp, and at the end they are glazed with warm honey. Many sources link it to San Giovanni in Fiore, in the Sila. Others say it was born between Catanzaro and Crotone, where it is also called *pitta 'nchiusa*. In the past it was also made for weddings and family celebrations. It takes patience, but it keeps for weeks.

**Makes:** 1 cake of 26 cm (10 in) · **Prep:** 1 hour 15 minutes · **Cook:** 45-50 minutes · **Rest:** 30 minutes · **Total:** about 2 hours 30 minutes · **Difficulty:** challenging

## Ingredients

**For the pastry**
- 500 g (1.1 lb) Italian 00 flour, or plain/all-purpose flour
- 100 ml (½ cup) mild extra virgin olive oil, or 90 g (3 oz) lard (recipe 06)
- 120 ml (½ cup) white or sweet muscat wine
- 60 ml (¼ cup) orange juice
- 50 g (2 oz) sugar
- 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon
- 1 pinch salt

**For the filling**
- 250 g (9 oz) shelled walnuts, coarsely chopped
- 100 g (3½ oz) toasted almonds, coarsely chopped
- 200 g (7 oz) raisins
- 150 g (5½ oz) honey (wildflower or chestnut)
- grated zest of 2 unwaxed oranges
- 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon
- 1 pinch ground cloves
- 2 tablespoons anise liqueur or sweet wine, optional

**To finish**
- 100 g (3½ oz) honey

**Equipment:** 26 cm (10 in) round tin, rolling pin or pasta machine, pastry wheel.

## Method

**The filling**
1. Soak the raisins in warm water (or in the sweet wine) for 30 minutes, then squeeze them.
2. Mix the walnuts, almonds, raisins, orange zest, cinnamon, cloves and liqueur.
3. Warm the honey just until runny and pour it over the filling. Mix.

**The pastry**
4. Mix the flour, sugar, cinnamon and salt.
5. Warm the oil, wine and orange juice until lukewarm and pour them over the flour.
6. Knead for 5-6 minutes, until the dough is smooth, elastic and not sticky. Cover it and leave it to rest for 30 minutes.
7. Heat the oven to 180 °C (355 °F), conventional (no fan). Oil the tin.

**Shaping**
8. Divide the dough into 4 pieces. Roll them out one at a time to 2 mm (⅛ in) thick (on a pasta machine, the second-to-last setting).
9. Cut the sheet into strips 6 cm (2¼ in) wide and about 40 cm (16 in) long.
10. Spread a spoonful of filling along the middle of each strip.
11. Fold the edges upwards and pinch the dough every 3-4 cm (1¼-1½ in), so the filling stays closed underneath and open on top.
12. Roll the strip up loosely into a little rose.
13. Put the first rose in the middle of the tin and arrange the others around it, packed tightly.
14. Spoon any leftover filling into the gaps between the roses.

**Baking**
15. Bake for 45-50 minutes, until the pastry is golden and the filling has small bubbles.
16. Warm the honey for finishing and brush it over the cake while still hot.
17. Let it cool completely in the tin before turning it out.

## Tips
- If the dough dries while you work, keep it covered with a tea towel.
- The cake is even better after 1-2 days, when the honey has been absorbed.

## Common mistakes
- Don't roll the pastry too thick: in the oven it stays hard and raw inside.
- Don't roll the roses too tight: in the oven they swell and split.

## Local variations
- With chopped dried figs in the filling.
- With lard instead of oil in the pastry.
- Instead of honey for finishing, fig honey (recipe 16).

## Storage
Covered with a tea towel or in a tin, in a cool place, for 2-3 weeks.

## Substitutions
The ingredients are available everywhere. Any sweet white wine works.

## Allergens (14 EU allergens)
Gluten (flour), tree nuts (walnuts, almonds), sulphites (wine, raisins).

## PDO and PGI
None.

## Wine
A sweet Calabrian wine, such as **Greco di Bianco DOC**.
