# Home-cured meats

In Calabria the pig is butchered in winter, when the cold makes the meat easier to work. A single animal gives sausage, soppressata, capocollo, pancetta, 'nduja, lard and cracklings. The recipes in this chapter follow traditional cuts and seasonings, and they take salt levels and curing times from the specifications of the Calabrian PDO cured meats. But they use today's tools, because a city kitchen is not a village cellar. What you make at home will be good and it will be yours, but it can never be called PDO: that name belongs only to certified producers.

## Safety rules for all raw cured meats

1. **Inspected meat.** Buy pork from a butcher, from pigs slaughtered under veterinary inspection (including the test for *Trichinella*). Never use meat from uninspected animals.
2. **Cold.** Keep meat, fat and equipment at 0-4 °C (32-39 °F) until you work them. Work in a cool room.
3. **Cleanliness.** Wash and dry the grinder, knives, stuffer, bowls and work surfaces before and after.
4. **Curing salt in dry-cured meats.** Nitrite protects against botulism. Always calculate it (see below) and never exceed the legal limit.
5. **Measure, don't guess.** Weigh everything on a scale accurate to 1 g. Check the temperature and humidity of your curing space with a thermo-hygrometer, and the weight loss with your scale.
6. **When in doubt, throw it out.** A rotten or sharply sour smell, green, black or fuzzy mould, or sticky, soft patches inside: do not eat it.

## How to calculate the curing salt

Curing salt is table salt with a small amount of sodium nitrite. The percentage is printed on the label: EU nitrite curing salt usually contains 0.4-0.9%; US "Prague Powder #1" (Cure #1) contains 6.25%.

1. Read the nitrite percentage on your label.
2. Choose the legal limit for your product (EU, Regulation 2023/2108):
   - **raw sausages** (dry sausage, soppressata, 'nduja): **80 mg per kg**;
   - **whole dry-salted cuts** (capocollo, pancetta): **65 mg per kg**.

   `[TO BE VERIFIED: both limits in Annex II of Regulation (EU) 2023/2108]`. Outside the EU, follow your own country's rules.
3. Grams of curing salt per kg of meat = **limit ÷ (percentage × 10)**.
   *Example:* limit 80 mg/kg, salt at 0.6% → 80 ÷ 6 = 13.3 g (0.5 oz) per kg. With Cure #1 at 6.25%: 80 ÷ 62.5 = 1.3 g per kg.
4. Make up the rest of the salt with fine sea salt, up to the total amount given in the recipe.

## Equipment

- Kitchen scale accurate to 1 g; for curing salt and cultures, better a scale accurate to 0.1 g.
- Meat grinder with 4-5 mm (⅛-¼ in) and 8-12 mm (⅜-½ in) plates, and a sausage stuffer (or the grinder's stuffing tube).
- Natural casings, butcher's twine, a clean needle.
- A curing space: a cellar or a curing fridge that stays at **12-15 °C (54-59 °F)** with **75-85% humidity**, dark and ventilated. Check it with a thermo-hygrometer.
- For fermented sausages: starter cultures and a pH meter (strips or probe).

## Weight loss

Weigh each piece as soon as it is made and write the weight on a tag tied to the string. Weigh it again every week. The weight loss is:

**(starting weight − today's weight) ÷ starting weight × 100**

Each recipe tells you the weight loss at which the meat is ready. If the outside dries but the inside stays soft (*case hardening*), the air is too dry: raise the humidity and slow down.
