# Pork cracklings and lard

*Ciccioli e strutto · curcùci, frisuragli · all of Calabria · winter*

Nothing from the pig is thrown away, least of all the fat. It is cut into small pieces and melted slowly in a cauldron for hours. What comes out is lard, *strutto*, snow-white once cold, which for generations was the cooking fat for savoury dishes and sweets. What stays at the bottom are the cracklings, *ciccioli*, golden and crisp, which are salted, seasoned with chilli and eaten with bread, or worked into focaccia dough. Every village has its own name for them: in Reggio *curcùci*, around Cosenza *frisuragli*, and other names elsewhere. Here they are made in a home kitchen pot, slowly.

**Makes:** about 1.2 kg (2.6 lb) lard and 300 g (11 oz) cracklings · **Prep:** 30 minutes · **Cook:** 2 hours 30 minutes · **Rest:** 1 night · **Difficulty:** medium

## Ingredients

- 2 kg (4.4 lb) pork fat (back fat and jowl fat), ideally with some meat still attached
- 150 ml (¾ cup) water
- 10 g (0.4 oz) fine salt, for the cracklings
- 1 teaspoon ground Calabrian chilli, optional
- 2 bay leaves, optional

**Equipment:** heavy-based stainless-steel or cast-iron pot of at least 5 l (5¼ quarts); slotted spoon; fine sieve; clean cheesecloth; clean, dry glass jars; kitchen thermometer.

## Method

1. Remove the skin from the fat and cut it into 1-2 cm (⅜-¾ in) cubes. The more even they are, the more evenly they cook.
2. Put the cubes in the pot with the water and bay leaves.
3. Turn the heat to low. The water stops the fat sticking at first, then it evaporates.
4. Cook for 2 hours 30 minutes on low heat, stirring every 10-15 minutes with a wooden spoon. The fat should bubble gently, never fry hard. If you have a thermometer, stay between 110 and 130 °C (265 °F).
5. After about 2 hours the pieces turn golden and the liquid fat becomes clear. That is the right moment.
6. Turn off the heat and leave it to cool for 10 minutes.
7. Lift out the cracklings with the slotted spoon and put them in a sieve over a bowl.
8. Strain the liquid lard through the cheesecloth-lined sieve into the jars. Leave 1 cm (⅜ in) of space below the rim.
9. Let the jars cool uncovered, then close them. Overnight the lard turns white and firm.
10. Press the still-warm cracklings with the back of a spoon to squeeze out extra fat.
11. Season the cracklings with the salt and chilli. Mix and let them cool on paper towels.

## Tips
- A little meat attached to the fat gives tastier cracklings.
- White, odourless lard comes from low heat. On high heat it turns yellow and tastes burnt.

## Common mistakes
- Never leave the pot unattended, and keep children away: hot fat causes serious burns.
- Never pour water on hot fat, and never put out burning fat with water: cover the pot with a lid and turn off the gas.
- Don't close jars of hot lard: condensation forms, and the water makes it go rancid.

## Local variations
- Around Reggio, curcùci are a by-product of making frittole (recipe 41).
- In some homes the cracklings are pressed into a block in a mould and sliced.

- Cracklings: in the fridge, in a closed container, for 1-2 weeks. They can be frozen.

## Substitutions
Ask the butcher for "pork fat for rendering". Lard replaces butter in the pastry for the Christmas sweets, such as pitta 'mpigliata (recipe 55).
