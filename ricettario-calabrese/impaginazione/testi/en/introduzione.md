# The cooking of the putìga

In Calabria the *putìga* is the village shop. This book takes its name from it because it is about the same kind of cooking: home cooking, made with what is in the pantry, in the vegetable garden and in season.

There are 60 recipes in nine chapters. The book starts with home-cured pork and preserves, because many later recipes use them: 'nduja in the fileja, sun-dried tomatoes in pasta, lard in the sweets. Then come antipasti and home cooking, bread, hand-made pasta, meat and fish main courses, side dishes and festive sweets.

## How to use this book

- Every recipe gives the area, season, servings, times and difficulty. Read it all before you start.
- Quantities are in grams and millilitres, with US measures in brackets. A kitchen scale makes everything easier.
- The steps are numbered, one action at a time, with the signs to look for: colour, smell, texture.
- After the steps you will find tips, common mistakes, local variations, storage and substitutions for cooks outside Italy.
- Allergens are listed in every recipe according to the 14 EU allergens (Regulation (EU) 1169/2011).
- At the end of the book there is a glossary of dialect names, a conversion table and an index by ingredient.

## Safety

Vegetables preserved in oil or vinegar and raw cured meats can be dangerous if they are made the wrong way. The preserves follow the home-canning guidelines of the Istituto Superiore di Sanità (the Italian National Institute of Health) and the Italian Ministry of Health. The rules for cured meats are at the start of their chapter. When a traditional method is no longer safe or no longer allowed, we tell it as history and give today's version.
